## Day 263: The Photo Archive and the Event-Guide Tool (June 21, 2026)

### IH20: One Database, 4,609 Photos

**Commits `e77705b78`, `805a7b4ca`, `4c92d8e32`, `f401accf5`, `a604f1a25`, `f5672e1cf`**

The Indy Hall photo archive had lived in two separate places — Flickr exports and Dropbox batch uploads — with no unified view. You consolidated them into a single SQLite database (`photos.db`) covering all 4,609 photos, then built a DB-driven picker that generates a browseable HTML page organized by 33 section categories.

The picker revealed how rich the archive was: 1,869 photos surfaced in the initial pass, with sections covering specific events, eras, and spaces across all four Indy Hall addresses. A photos README documented the architecture, workflow, and full section inventory. The Playbook ingest and photo dating skills added tools for associating photos with specific events. Camera Uploads 2011-2012 — a previously uncategorized batch — got a vision review workflow to date and categorize them.

**Lesson Learned:** Unifying fragmented archives isn't just organization work — it's precondition work. Until `photos.db` existed, you couldn't know you had 4,609 photos across 33 categories. The inventory reveals what's possible.

### Event-Guide: From Sketch to CLI in One Session

**Commits `b622b24cf`, `4c2b1ba3e`, `e053341b3`, `449e8c627`, `d7e4c1f2d`, `b1eb37c30`, and 5 more**

A new `event-runsheet` skill shipped alongside a full TypeScript CLI (`event-guide`) that builds structured event guides from a component library. The architecture: reusable components (venue details, room access, catering, A/V, parking) combine into event-specific guides, with two presets shipping day one — HOP (House of Poets) and the 4th-floor Skyroom configuration pulled from a Deidre-Odi guide.

The CLI grew through the session: a `--save-preset` flag to lock in a proven configuration, a `--validate` pass before committing, an event log for tracking builds, `--add` for ad-hoc component insertion, a QR code in the output, and time/notes/point-person/staff flag fields. By EOD a Deidre-Odi guide had been generated from the Skyroom preset — the first real output.

**Lesson Learned:** Component-based document generation makes the second guide take 10% of the time the first one took. The preset isn't just a shortcut — it's accumulated institutional knowledge made reusable.

### Network Tracker: Co-Presence (GH #257)

**Commits `50ee52e40`, `7cb58f773`, `c3aa033ec`, `db4892245`, `6b780c7ec`, `106c59174`, `af697beae`, `a273b300f`**

The Network Tracker's most-requested feature landed: a Co-Presence tab showing which members consistently arrive on the same days. The backend computes pairwise co-occurrence over a 90-day rolling window using `checkin_events`, filters to members only (no visitors), requires ≥3 shared days, and returns the top 20 pairs ranked by frequency.

From there, the feature evolved quickly. Jaccard weighting replaced raw frequency counts to normalize for members who check in constantly — otherwise, high-frequency members dominate every pair. An Unknown Regulars view surfaced members with 10+ check-ins who don't appear in the members table at all: people attending regularly without formal membership. The Pairs and Isolated views were removed after a review council audit: the co-presence data is hub-and-spoke, not clustered, and those views weren't adding signal.

Mobile bottom nav, 24h response caching, cache invalidation on new check-ins, pre-warm 800ms after page load, tap-to-retry on timeout, and a pinch/tap zoom disable for mobile rounded out the polish. A full README landed covering architecture, setup, API endpoints, DB schema, and a scheduled-jobs section documenting the gap in DB cleanup automation.

**Lesson Learned:** Raw co-occurrence frequency always overweights high-frequency attenders. Jaccard normalization is the correct first move for any co-presence dataset where individual attendance rates vary widely.

### Micro-Garage: The Mudsill Decision

**Commits `15ec30e21`, `8590c0c32`, `06543facc`, `02b550103`, `436f3e5e9`, `3ba251799`**

A structural conflict that had been implicit in the build plan got resolved: sheathing and Tapcon anchors can't coexist at the same base layer. The double-bottom mudsill approach separates the slab-anchoring function from the wall-framing function. PT mudsills get Tapcon'd to the slab first as a dedicated anchor layer. Wall frames get their own KD bottom plates that drop onto the pre-set mudsills after raising.

This propagated through the whole cut list: full studs shortened 73.5"→72" (maintaining 78" wall height from slab), PT vs KD distinction clarified throughout, cellar-zone studs unchanged at 40.5". A Mudsill view mode was added to the JSCAD 3D viewer. The Shopping tab added two trip checklists with localStorage persistence.

**Lesson Learned:** Structural decisions have downstream consequences that aren't visible until you propagate them through the full cut list. The parametric model made the propagation automatic.

## Day 264: The Cork Board (June 22, 2026)

### AI Caption Pipeline: 4,609 Photos, Searchable

**Commit `f56dd0902`**

Before building anything visual with the photo archive, you solved the metadata problem. A TypeScript caption pipeline (`caption-photos.ts`) runs vision captioning on each photo via jfdi, writing results to a `photo_captions` table with versioned output: title, caption, keywords, enriched_date, enriched_location, source_trail, and confidence score.

The detail that unlocked quality: a `photo-location-context.json` file mapping Indy Hall's era to address — 32 Strawberry St (2006-2009), 22 N 3rd St (2009-2016), 399 Market St (2016-2022), 709 N 2nd St (2022-present). Injecting this into the prompt gave the vision model enough context to assign location and date with confidence, pushing from 0.82 to 0.93 on the first test.

**Lesson Learned:** Vision models captioning archival photos need temporal and geographic anchors to produce confident, accurate metadata. "Photo from a coworking space" is a 0.82. "Photo from Indy Hall at 399 Market St, probably 2016-2022" is a 0.93.

### The Cork Board: 40+ Commits, One Day

**Commits `c22ad5ac2`, `71ddbc794`, `15002d24c`, `0abadc9d6`, `d1c70b10f`, `7c2576842`, `789f671cd`, `4c35a4551`, `bf1ef85f7`, `076a94c76`, `6c35280c7`, `2aadc182e`, `6963bb52b`, and 30+ more**

The IH20 party invite needed a page that felt like standing in front of 20 years of Indy Hall history. You built it in a day.

The cork board started as an infinite canvas (`scripts/cork-board.html`) with 400 randomly-selected photos from the archive served as polaroids from a static JSON pool on DO Spaces — thumbnails at low zoom, full-res originals swapping in automatically at 1.5× zoom. From there, a 40+ commit sprint ran through every detail:

**Canvas mechanics:**
- Grid-based placement to reduce overlap; consistent position per photo (shuffle reverted twice before grid placement landed)
- URL hash encoding pan+zoom for shareable links; click copies link, hash link animates to position, direct navigation snaps instantly
- Click-to-front layering
- Intro zoom-out-to-zoom-in animation on load (added → reverted → restored with random landing position)
- Auto-zoom to a random location on load; save position after drawer close; random drop always lands on a photo

**Visual identity:**
- Hand-drawn Caveat font year captions on each polaroid
- Rounded top edge with white strip above the cork board; straight top edge for the board itself
- White theme-color, body background white

**Lightbox:**
- Tap a polaroid → full-res lightbox as a polaroid card with thick white frame and caption in the writing area
- Caption and year below the photo; consistent minimum size so small photos don't collapse
- Spinner while loading; pinch-zoom routes to lightbox when open
- Only closes on backdrop tap, not on photo tap; 300ms click fallthrough suppressed when closing

**Bottom drawer:**
- Drag-to-open/close with a rounded tab and layered shadow
- Opens to 50vh by default on page load; tab tap toggles open/close
- Party invite panel inside; Tailwind CDN, border polish

**Lesson Learned:** A 40-commit feature sprint on a single HTML file isn't scope creep — it's the difference between a prototype and something you're proud to send. Each commit made the next one obvious.

## Reflection: Week 35

---

### The Mistakes We Made

#### 1. Photo Shuffle Was Added and Reverted Twice

**Cost:** `edb47b334` and `c7a2f16d3` both revert shuffle — the same finding needed twice.\
**Fix:** Grid-based deterministic placement so each photo has a consistent position.\
**Lesson Learned:** On an infinite canvas, consistency matters more than novelty. Users return to the same page expecting to find photos where they left them.

#### 2. The Intro Animation Was Added, Reverted, and Restored

**Cost:** The zoom-out-to-zoom-in animation was committed, then reverted (`1ea859154`), then restored in a later form — a sign that the intent wasn't fully clear before building it.\
**Fix:** The restored version tied the animation to a random landing position, giving it functional purpose beyond decoration.\
**Lesson Learned:** Animations that exist purely for effect are easy to revert. Animations that communicate something (where you landed, what the scale of the board is) earn their place.

---

### The Surprising Things

#### 1. Location Context Jumped Caption Confidence by 11 Points

**Expected:** Vision captioning of archival photos would produce reasonable but vague results — the model would recognize coworking spaces but not specific addresses or eras.\
**Actual:** A 4-entry era→address JSON (32 lines) pushed confidence from 0.82 to 0.93 on the first test. The model already knew roughly what Indy Hall was and when photos were taken. What it needed was the specific address at each era to make the location claim with confidence.

A tiny lookup table did what more training data couldn't.

#### 2. Unknown Regulars Was More Useful Than Co-Presence Pairs

**Expected:** Co-presence pairs ranked by frequency would be the core useful view from the co-presence graph.\
**Actual:** After a review council audit, Pairs and Isolated views were removed — the data is hub-and-spoke, not clustered, and pairs just showed the same high-frequency members everywhere. The most useful view turned out to be Unknown Regulars: members attending 10+ times who don't appear in the formal members table.

The feature built to show relationships revealed a population that didn't exist in the official record.

#### 3. The Event-Guide Component System Appeared Fully Formed

**Expected:** An event-guide tool would require multiple sessions to reach a usable state.\
**Actual:** The component system, two presets, event log, QR code, --save-preset, --validate, --add, and a complete SKILL.md all shipped in a single June 21 session. The first real guide (Deidre-Odi, Skyroom) was generated the same day.

When you have concrete examples already in hand (existing event runsheets), the abstraction is discoverable in one pass.
