### Slow First Load, Fixed by Reading Less (GH #1814)

**Commit `f10cc8d322`**

Alex said on Friday that the first load was always slow. The deck read each card from Discord one at a time: 13 cards took 39 seconds, and half the reads waited about 5 seconds on Discord's read limit. One 100-message read now covers cards posted close together, and each message caches for five minutes. Cold load is 1.8 seconds, warm is 30 to 50 ms. **Lesson Learned:** The rate limit was per channel, so parallel reads fought each other. One wide read beat 13 narrow ones.

## Day 416: Logins Expire, Instagram Says Stop, and Triage Gets Its Card Types (November 21, 2026)

### All Three Claude Logins Expired (GH #1815, #1816)

**Commits `0e5473e76e`, `40ea5cb108`, `477f29e06e`, `3c2e6335f2`, `ad6ae423e5`, `14d6ed0dc3`, `7f56167a56`**

Browser logins in the claude-rotate proxy die after about 30 days with `invalid_grant`. By 11:34 UTC all three had expired. jfdi falls back only when `/v1/models` fails its check, so jobs that asked for a listed model hit dead auth. The scheduler was put on a direct login with `JOB_PROXY=direct`. An empty string would have done nothing, since PM2 drops it entirely and the claude-rotate default stays on. `pm2-safe-restart --config` also read a hard-coded env copy that never had `JOB_PROXY`, so it now reads env from `ecosystem.config.cjs`. By 12:13 UTC all three accounts were on one-year `claude setup-token` credentials, the proxy ran its own binary on CLIProxyAPI 8.0.80, and 12 of 12 test requests returned 200. Scheduled jobs went back on claude-rotate. Job alerts now name the API error instead of "jfdi cleanup".

**Lesson Learned:** A credential that expires on a schedule needs a tripwire before it expires. Setup tokens last a year, so the next tripwire belongs in the calendar.

### A Missing Transcript Stopped Looping (GH #1824)

**Commit `96498922f2`**

`ResumableSessionGuard` degraded to a fresh session only when a database restore was attempted and failed. When `transcript_path` pointed at a missing file and the database held no copy either, it fell through and passed `--resume`. Claude exited with "No conversation found", jfdi waited out its startup timeout, and the bridge posted a fake "Anthropic API error". The ICP S2 thread hit it four times. The fresh session now gets the restore note and the thread's seed snapshot. Only 39 of about 82,500 sessions from 14 to 60 days ago had no database copy.

**Lesson Learned:** An error shown to the user must come from the failure that happened. A canned "API error" hid a missing file for four retries.

## Reflection: Week 57

---

### The Mistakes We Made

#### 1. Three Logins Expired Together

All three claude-rotate accounts used browser logins that die after about 30 days. They expired within hours of each other on Nov 21 (`0e5473e76e`, `40ea5cb108`, `3c2e6335f2`).\
**Lesson Learned:** Accounts set up on the same day expire on the same day. Stagger them or use credentials with a year of life, and note the renewal date.

#### 2. An Error Message Told the Wrong Story

A thread with no transcript anywhere passed `--resume`, and the bridge posted a canned "Anthropic API error" four times (`96498922f2`, #1824).\
**Lesson Learned:** A canned message hides the real cause. Name the failure that happened, even when it is a missing file.

#### 3. Scraping a Personal Account Until It Warned

Instagram logged out both browsers and then said it suspected automated behavior (`59600e77d2`, #1823).\
**Lesson Learned:** A warning from a platform is the last cheap signal. Alex's call to stop was the right one.

#### 4. A Job Nobody Read Cost Swap

`track-claude-usage` pushed 2 to 4 GB into swap every hour for outputs with no reader (`cf7c787e5b`, #1764).\
**Lesson Learned:** Check who reads a job's output before tuning the job.

#### 5. Built, Tried, Reverted

The Island-grown turn status, the step emoji in the bar, the rose needle on the vibe knob, help desk ticket pickup, photo b-roll for ICP, and Portless previews were each built and reverted within a day (`42b1a5b600`, `af6f672247`, `825c06bdbe`, `ac8c84944d`, `90d6520c09`, `d83212e58a`).\
**Lesson Learned:** Cheap to build, cheap to remove. The team's pace of trying things depends on the revert being one commit.

### The Surprising Things

#### 1. Parallel Reads Made a Slow Load Slower

**Expected:** 13 Discord reads fired at once would beat 13 in a row.\
**Actual:** Four timed out at 30 seconds, because the rate limit is per channel. One 100-message read took the load from 39 seconds to 1.8.

#### 2. An Editor Replaced Its Own Preview in One Day

**Expected:** The live preview next to the draft would stay as the main view.\
**Actual:** On Day 412 the preview became the editor, with History, undo and Kit publishing added the same day.

#### 3. Twenty Site Ideas Got Decided in One Day

**Expected:** A redesign would need rounds of review.\
**Actual:** Four prototype batches and one decisions log settled which old pages retire, redirect or stay.

---

### Lessons for Building AI Systems

- **Shadow mode first, then enforce.** Two weeks of logs showed one miss before the team key list went live.
- **Read-only before write.** The Chat tab shipped read-only on Nov 19 and two-way on Nov 20, and the email review room started read-only.
- **A reader makes a producer worth running.** The usage job went when no reader remained.
- **Name the actual failure.** Both "No conversation found" and the Instagram warning were the real signal that a canned message hid.
- **One wide read beats many narrow ones.** Check the rate limit's scope before parallelizing.
- **Advise, never block.** The duplicate-function hook names look-alikes and lets the commit through.
