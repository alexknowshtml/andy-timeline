## Overview: The Week the Pool Came Down

Week 31 ended with a confident forecast: the pane pool was live, and the next frontier was tuning it for capacity. Week 32 did the opposite. The pool was **fully decommissioned** — service, infrastructure, and every caller removed — and replaced by a simpler abstraction that had been quietly absorbing its work all along: **herdr** (a persistent Claude session manager) plus direct `jfdi` subprocess invocation routed through a single hardened invoker. By the time the container was torn down on Sunday, it had **zero active jobs**. Everything had already migrated.

That is the headline, and it carries the week's biggest lesson: you build the elaborate thing to learn what the problem actually needs, then you find the simpler thing and tear the elaborate thing down. The pool solved fork storms by holding sessions open in tmux panes behind an HTTP router. Herdr solved the same problem with less surface area — no proxy, no port, no debug UI to maintain. So the proxy layer came off Saturday (Day 241), and the whole service came down Sunday (Day 242) in a single clean decommission commit that deleted 26 files.

The decommission rode in on a broader **Claude CLI consolidation** — a 7-phase plan to route every direct `claude` call in `scripts/` through one invoker, harden each against shell injection, and add `maxTurns`/`disallowedTools` controls. Sunday was 139 commits, the busiest day of the week, knocking out herdr wiring, the pool teardown, a USE_JFDI migration of a dozen scheduled jobs, and a brand-new `/pay` skill for Mercury ACH payments.

Running parallel to all of that, the first half of the week was a **member-communications build**: a topic-based email preferences and segments system (per-topic opt-out toggles, `prefs.indyhall.org` on Netlify + Turso, daily Nexudus member sync), an iPostal subscriber UI with login-state detection, and an email **promise-detection → calendar-invite** workflow that turns "I'll send you that by Friday" into a tracked Discord button and a calendar hold. Plus a `travel-research` skill with a live fact-check pass, born from planning a Costa Rica trip.

**Key themes:** pane pool full decommission (built Week 31, gone Week 32), herdr + jfdi invoker consolidation, Claude CLI shell-injection hardening, USE_JFDI scheduled-job migration, `/pay` Mercury ACH skill, email preferences + segments system (`prefs.indyhall.org`), email promise-detection → calendar-invite workflow, iPostal subscriber UI, `travel-research` skill with live fact-check, lift-philly inbound email → Sheets + SMS.

## Reflection: Week 32

---

### The Mistakes We Made

#### 1. We Over-Built Before We Knew the Shape of the Problem

**Cost:** The Claude Pane Pool was a full week's marathon in Week 31 — a Podman container, an HTTP router, an SSE relay, a debug UI, crash recovery, a job queue. It ran in production for six days before being decommissioned with zero active callers.\
**Fix:** herdr — a smaller abstraction already in the codebase — absorbed the pool's job (eliminating fork storms) with far less surface area. The proxy came off Day 241, the service came down Day 242.\
**Lesson Learned:** The elaborate build wasn't wasted — it taught us exactly what the problem needed, which turned out to be less than we built. But the cheaper lesson would have been to try herdr first. Reach for the smallest abstraction that could work before building the comprehensive one.

#### 2. 2,655 Ghost Sessions

**Cost:** Sessions accumulated without TTL eviction until a cleanup script deleted 2,655 of them in one pass.\
**Fix:** A TTL-eviction cleanup script plus the broader move toward herdr-managed sessions.\
**Lesson Learned:** Anything that creates records needs a retention policy from day one. "We'll clean it up later" is how you end up deleting 2,655 of something.

---

### The Surprising Things

#### 1. The Decommission Was Boring — On Purpose

**Expected:** Tearing down a week-old production service that routed all scheduled jobs would be a risky, multi-step migration with rollback anxiety.\
**Actual:** It was a single commit deleting 26 files, because every job had already quietly migrated to herdr. `pool-routing.json` listed zero active routes.

The surprise was the absence of drama. The migration had already happened incrementally; the decommission was just acknowledging it.

#### 2. The Live Fact-Check Caught Its Own First Draft

**Expected:** A fact-check step on the travel-research skill would mostly be a safety net that rarely fires.\
**Actual:** It immediately caught pricing and room-comparison errors in the very first Costa Rica research draft it ran against.

Research output is confidently wrong often enough that the check earns its place on the first run, not the hundredth.
