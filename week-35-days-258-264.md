## Days 258-260: The Bridge Decomposition (June 16-18, 2026)

### The Bridge Decomposition — GH #62 Runs Three Days

**June 16 commits `e07bf35664`, `7255cb2558` (and duplicates)**

The systematic dismantling of the Discord bridge started on June 16 with bot.ts split into focused modules (GH #200) and bot-button-handlers.ts split into 6 focused modules (GH #201). The goal of GH #62 was to take the monolithic Discord bridge and give each concern its own file.

June 17 ran ten more extractions in a single session: bot-event-handlers.ts extracted (step 1), InteractionCreate moved to bot-event-handlers (step 2), then bot-event-handlers.ts itself (1,294 lines) split into 5 focused modules. The HTTP layer followed: pure HTTP utils extracted to http-utils.ts, shared server state to server-state.ts, GET query handlers to http-handlers-query.ts, suggest-options handlers to http-handlers-suggest.ts, messaging handlers to http-handlers-messaging.ts, thread handlers and closeThread to dedicated modules, and /command handler to http-handlers-command.ts. All as-any casts removed (GH #204).

June 18 completed the decomposition: subagent/handoff/session routes extracted to subagent-endpoints.ts, Discord endpoint routes to discord-endpoints.ts, async session loader to async-sessions.ts, claude/memory/graph/embed routes to claude-endpoints.ts, and health/claude/job/entity routes to their own modules. The critical commit: spawn-claude-async dispatch wired through the new modules, dropping a 1,491-line inline duplicate. spawn-claude-stream followed (194-line inline dropped). The same day, chat-server completed its own decomposition (GH #217): tool-display.ts, media-processor.ts, session-reader.ts, context-analyzer.ts, chat-state.ts, dangerous-commands-state.ts, broadcast.ts, server-state.ts, stream-chat.ts, and finally routes.ts — "thin entry point complete." The spawn-handlers joined the pattern: handleSpawnClaudeAsync, handleSpawnClaude, handleSpawnClaudeStream, and fireCompletionCallback each got their own modules.

**Lesson Learned:** The pattern locked in after the first two extractions and each subsequent module took 3-4 minutes. Once you have the first working example of the shape you're extracting, the remaining extractions become mechanical. The value isn't in any single extraction — it's in having a route table with 10 focused files you can read independently instead of one 1,500-line file you have to hold in your head.

## Day 263: IH20, Co-Presence, Micro-Garage, Cork Board Begins (June 21, 2026)

### IH20: One Database, 4,609 Photos

**Commits `364b4980e6`, `cbbeb74277`, `3be3ed3002`, `cbc18e6543`, `254bf08d06`**

The photo archive expanded to 33 sections and 1,869 photos in the picker. A TwitPic Archive (423 photos, originals only) was added — photos from the 2009-2012 era that predated Flickr's dominance. Playbook ingest and photo dating skills added tools for associating photos with specific events. Camera Uploads 2011-2012 — previously uncategorized — got a vision review workflow. An AI caption pipeline (`caption-photos`) shipped, writing to a `photo_captions` table with title, caption, keywords, enriched_date, enriched_location, and confidence score.

The caption quality breakthrough: a `photo-location-context.json` mapping Indy Hall's era to address (32 Strawberry St 2006-2009, 22 N 3rd St 2009-2016, 399 Market St 2016-2022, 709 N 2nd St 2022-present) injected into the vision prompt pushed confidence from 0.82 to 0.93 on the first test.

**Lesson Learned:** Vision models captioning archival photos need temporal and geographic anchors to produce confident, accurate metadata. "Photo from a coworking space" is 0.82. "Photo from Indy Hall at 399 Market St, probably 2016-2022" is 0.93. A 32-line lookup file did what more training data couldn't.

### Network Tracker: Co-Presence (GH #257)

**Commits `2d87dfddca`, `17824ac835`, `9f0d176892`, `a480796e36`, `2b37fee3d3`, `fa67a92b76`, and 8 more**

The co-presence tab landed: pairwise co-occurrence over a 90-day rolling window, members only, minimum 3 shared days, top 20 pairs. Jaccard weighting replaced raw frequency counts immediately — otherwise high-frequency members dominate every pair. An Unknown Regulars view surfaced members with 10+ check-ins not in the formal members table.

After a review pass, Pairs and Isolated views were removed — the data is hub-and-spoke, not clustered, and those views weren't adding signal. **Lesson Learned:** Raw co-occurrence frequency always overweights high-frequency attenders. Jaccard normalization is the correct first move for any co-presence dataset where individual attendance rates vary widely.

## Reflection: Week 35

---

### The Mistakes We Made

#### 1. Five Active Days Called "Data Syncs Only"

The week's original narrative described June 16-20 as quiet days with data syncs. In reality: June 18 alone logged 320 commits — the biggest single day of the week — completing two parallel monolith decompositions (bridge + chat-server). The first five days were architecture and infrastructure days, not rest days.

**Why it happened:** The visual builds (cork board, photo archive) were the most shareable story. The decomposition work was load-bearing but less immediately visible.

**Lesson Learned:** Low-drama days are often the highest-impact days. Decomposition work doesn't generate the same kind of visible features as a new UI, but eliminating a 1,491-line inline duplicate is real value.

#### 2. Intro Animation Added, Reverted, Not Restored

The zoom-out-to-zoom-in intro animation was committed and then reverted on June 21. Unlike in a prior commit message, it was not restored — the revert was final.

**Lesson Learned:** Animations that exist purely for effect are easy to revert. Build the functional version before the decorative version.

---

### The Surprising Things

#### 1. 320 Commits in One Day — and Most Were Refactors

June 18 was the biggest single day of the week at 320 commits. Most of them were refactors: module extractions that deleted more lines than they added (the spawn-claude-async wiring dropped 1,491 inline lines; the chat-server decomposition broke a monolith into a thin routes.ts entry point). High commit volume without shipping new features — that's architecture.

#### 2. Location Context Jumped Caption Confidence by 11 Points

A 4-entry era→address JSON (32 lines) pushed vision captioning confidence from 0.82 to 0.93 on the first test. The model already knew roughly what Indy Hall was. What it needed was the specific address at each era to make the location claim with confidence.

A tiny lookup table did what more training data couldn't.

#### 3. Unknown Regulars Was More Useful Than Co-Presence Pairs

After a review pass, the Pairs and Isolated views were removed from the co-presence graph. The most useful view turned out to be Unknown Regulars: members attending 10+ times who don't appear in the formal members table. The feature built to show relationships revealed a population that didn't exist in the official record.

#### 4. The Event-Guide Component System Appeared Fully Formed

The component system, two presets, event log, QR code, --save-preset, --validate, --add, and a complete SKILL.md all shipped across June 20-21. The first real guide (Deidre-Odi, Skyroom) was generated the same day. When you have concrete examples already in hand (existing event runsheets), the abstraction is discoverable in one pass.
